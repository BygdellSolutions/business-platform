# Changelog

All notable changes to `business-platform` will be documented in this file.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Inventory, I1: stock ledger and lifecycle effects.** Items gain an optional article number (`sku`, unique within the organization) and `track_stock` (products only; the database refuses it on a service, also after a change of type). A new Inventory module (`app/modules/inventory`) keeps an append-only, tenant-owned `stock_movements` ledger: quantity before, change and after, reason (opening, adjustment, receipt, delivery, return), note, who and when; CHECKs keep the arithmetic right and stock never below zero, and a trigger refuses UPDATE and DELETE except while the organization is being deleted. The latest movement's "after" IS the quantity on hand; every writer holds the item's row lock. `GET /api/items/{id}/stock` (any member) and `POST /api/items/{id}/stock` (record writers: a count, or stock added or removed; the first count is the opening stock, every later change needs a note). The item page shows a Stock panel for a product that tracks stock. Core's lifecycle seam gains **effects**: after a transaction's step is written, registered effects act in the same database transaction under the same row lock, after the validators; one that refuses rolls the whole step back (Inventory's own effects come with I3). An item with stock movements cannot be deleted. Migration `a8c0e2f4b679` (additive).
- **Service lines.** Add line offers Catalog item / Service / Ad-hoc, in that order. A service line is work performed for someone or something: a service from the catalog (products are refused), for whom (any registered *service subject*: a horse or a customer today, `service_subject=True` in the registry; resolved in the active organization, inactive refused), performed by (a member, chosen from `GET /api/members/people`), performed at (local time in the organization's time zone; empty means now) and notes. It is priced exactly like a catalog line, discounts included. `transaction_lines` gains `kind`, `performed_at`, `performed_by`, `subject_type`, `subject_id`, `notes` with CHECKs that a service has a subject and a time and a standard line has neither; invoices keep a snapshot of the service (`invoice_lines.service`: time as the organization read it, performer and subject names, notes) that is never resolved again. `GET /api/transactions/services` lists services for a subject or a billing customer; the horse page shows the services performed on the horse and the customer page the services billed to the customer. A horse or customer named on a service line cannot be deleted (reference guard). Migration `f7b9d1e3a568` (additive; existing lines become `standard`).
- **Discounts.** A customer's permanent discount (`customers.default_discount_percent`, owners and admins only, in the history) and temporary catalog discounts per item (`item_discounts`: percent, from, to, note; periods never overlap; owners and admins; `GET/POST /api/items/{id}/discounts`, `DELETE .../{discount_id}`; items show the discount active today). A catalog line is priced in ordered layers (`pricing.discounted_unit_price`): the item's price, then the campaign active on the transaction's date, then the customer's discount, each rounded half-up to two decimals before the next (1 000.00 -20 % -10 % = 720.00, never 30 % off). Lines store `list_unit_price`, `catalog_discount_percent` and `customer_discount_percent` next to the final `unit_price_ex_vat`, and a CHECK on transaction and invoice lines enforces the same rule; a typed price or an ad-hoc line carries no discounts; a new billing customer or date reprices the draft's catalog lines (recorded in the history); invoices copy the layers verbatim. Lines and invoices show every step. Migration `e5a7c9d1f246` (additive; no past line gets a discount).
- **Owned-organization limit** ("Owned 1 / 1"). `users.max_owned_organizations` (default 1; migration `d4f6b8c0e135` gives existing accounts max(1, what they own) and drops the yes/no `can_create_organizations`) counts owner memberships only. Every path to ownership checks it under the user-row lock (`app/core/ownership.py`): creating an organization, promotion to owner, ownership transfer, and accepting an owner invitation (refused whole with "You cannot become an owner of this organization because you have reached your owned-organization limit."; the invitation stays pending). A database trigger repeats the check for any path that forgets it; the operator's `repair owner` is the one override. `GET /api/me/user` reports `owned_organizations` / `max_owned_organizations` (and the computed `can_create_organizations`); the organization switcher shows "Owned X / Y". Operator: `set-owned-limit --email --limit N`; `grant-org-creation` / `revoke-org-creation` keep working (room for one more / exactly what is owned now).
- **Danger zone and account security.** A Danger Zone at the bottom of Organization Settings replaces the header's Leave button: leave (any member; the sole owner is told to transfer ownership or delete instead), transfer ownership (owner: the member becomes owner, the previous owner becomes admin; `POST /api/organization/transfer-ownership`) and delete the organization (owner: a HARD delete of everything it owns, issued invoices, PDFs and history included, after a warning that also names the Swedish retention rules, the organization's name typed and an acknowledgement; `POST /api/organization/delete`). All three require **recent authentication** (`app/core/reauth.py`: today the password, throttled and recorded as `reauth_failure`; the development sign-in has no passwords and is exempt). Deletion passes the issued-invoice, PDF and history protections only for the organization being deleted (transaction-local `app.deleting_organization`, migration `c3e5a7b9d024`), and a test keeps its table list equal to every table with an `organization_id`. A new **Account security** page (`/account`, linked from the header) changes the signed-in person's password through a CSRF-protected BFF route; other sessions end, this one stays.
- **History: who changed what, when, and what it was before.** An append-only, tenant-owned `audit_events` table (migration `b2d4f6a8c013`; a trigger refuses UPDATE and DELETE except while its own organization is being deleted) records every create, change, delete and lifecycle step of customers, catalog items, horses, transactions and their lines, custom-field values and invoices (created, draft changes, issued), in the same database transaction as the change, with the old and new values as stored. `created_by` / `updated_by` on those records (NULL = not recorded for older rows; nothing is guessed). `GET /api/history?entity_type&entity_id` (any member; a transaction includes its lines). Detail pages show "Created … by … · Last changed … by …" and a History list in the organization's time zone with names instead of ids; invoices also show who issued them.
- **Organization time zone.** Owners and admins set an IANA time zone in Settings (`organizations.timezone`, migration `a1c3e5f7b902`; validated against the bundled `tzdata` database, which is now an explicit dependency so the slim production image has it). New transactions and invoices created without a date get the organization's own date (`app/core/org_time.py`, through the clock seam), and the new-transaction form is prefilled with it (`today` in `GET /api/organization`) instead of the browser's date. Existing organizations are not backfilled: without a zone, dates default to UTC exactly as before, and Settings says so.

Every tool takes its database from one explicit variable (never `DATABASE_URL`), passes the password only in `PGPASSWORD`, and scrubs client output (whole URLs, hosts, passwords).

### Security
- **Viewers are read-only for business records.** Before, any member (viewers included) could create, change and delete customers, catalog items and horses, create and edit transactions and their lines, complete/reopen/cancel them and write custom-field values. These writes now require the owner, admin, accountant or employee role (`record_writer` in `app/core/authz.py`); a viewer gets 403 and nothing changes, judged before any lookup so foreign and random ids answer the same. A route inventory test fails if a tenant-scoped write route has no role rule or admits viewers. The frontend shows viewers read-only detail views, no create links, a "not allowed" page instead of create forms and a read-only transaction editor (presentation only). This changes the earlier decision that every member may write custom-field values.

### Changed
- A default account may now create its first organization (limit 1), including invited members; before, creating needed the operator's explicit right. Retrying a creation that already committed returns it even when the limit has since been reached or lowered (it creates nothing new); before, a revoked right refused the replay.
- `APP_ENV` has no default in the backend (a process that was not told its environment does not start); `PUBLIC_ORIGIN` and `CORS_ORIGINS` default only in development; `app.core.db.Base` moved to `app.core.base` (re-exported); the container commands are `--no-access-log --no-proxy-headers --no-server-header`; every response of the backend carries `x-request-id`.
- `next.config.ts`: `output: "standalone"`, `poweredByHeader: false`.
- Production frontend now EXITS (status 1) on an unusable authentication configuration; before, a standalone server only logged the failure and kept answering every request with a 500 (which a connect-only health check calls healthy).
- **Production authentication, S5: invitations** (hardening and documentation are the last slice). An owner or admin invites a person by email and role; there is no email delivery: the administrator copies a link `/invite#<token>` that is shown once (`POST /api/invitations` returns the 256-bit token once; only its SHA-256 is stored; no list, event or log ever carries it; regenerate = revoke and issue a new token). Tenant-scoped `GET/POST /api/invitations`, `POST /api/invitations/{id}/regenerate`, `DELETE /api/invitations/{id}` (revoke is a lifecycle update, idempotent, and never removes a membership; an accepted invitation cannot be revoked).
- **Authority** from fresh locked rows (S4's membership lock, then the invitation row): an owner invites any role and revokes anything; an admin only accountant/employee/viewer; other roles nothing. At most one pending invitation per organization and email (a partial unique index that does not mention expiry; an expired one is superseded explicitly by the next create); inviting an existing member is refused.
- **Acceptance** is not organization-scoped: the token (in a POST body) locates the invitation, and the organization and role come only from its locked row. `POST /api/invite/preview` (pre-auth; organization name, invited email, role, whether the account exists), `POST /api/invite/accept` (signed in; the account's email must equal the invited one, otherwise a specific refusal that consumes nothing), `POST /api/invite/accept-new` (pre-auth, session mode: creates the user, credential, membership, session and the settled invitation in ONE transaction; a weak password or any failure consumes nothing). An invitation never changes an existing membership role; exactly one acceptance creates the membership (a retry by the same person is idempotent); every unusable token gets one generic answer.
- **Browser secret handling.** `/invite#<token>`: the fragment never reaches a server; the page reads it, removes it with `history.replaceState` at once, keeps it in memory only, sends it only in POST bodies (existing-account sign-in happens on the same page with the same in-memory token, no redirect or `next`), and is served `no-store` with `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`. Pre-authentication requests reuse S2's pre-auth double submit; the signed-in acceptance uses the ordinary session CSRF at both layers.
- **Frontend.** Invitations section on the Members page (role choices by apparent authority, the link shown once with a copy button and a one-time notice, regenerate, confirmed revoke) and the invite page (signed in as the invited account, signed in as another account with "sign out and continue", signed out with an account, signed out without one; ends in `/o/{id}`).
- **Security events** `invitation_created`, `invitation_revoked`, `invitation_accepted` (ids and roles only); migration `e29c5d7a3b48` (additive, reversible): `organization_invitations` and the widened event-type CHECK. Setting `INVITATION_TTL_DAYS` (default 7).

- **Production authentication, S4: membership administration** (invitations are the next slice). `GET /api/members` (owner/admin; membership id, name, email, role, `is_you`; no user ids, credentials, sessions or capability flag), `PATCH /api/members/{membership_id}` (body: the role only), `DELETE /api/members/{membership_id}` and `POST /api/members/leave`, all tenant-scoped. Authority is decided by the backend: an owner may set any role, remove others and step down or leave while another owner remains; an admin may manage only accountants, employees and viewers, only to those roles; nobody else administers; nobody changes their own role except an owner stepping down; every member may leave; the generic removal refuses to remove oneself.
- **Fresh authority under lock.** Every mutation first locks ALL membership rows of the one organization in ascending id order (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` with `populate_existing`), then re-checks the actor's membership and current role, the target's membership and current role, the requested role and only then the owner count. One statement, one order: mutations of an organization serialize without deadlock; another organization's administration is never blocked. Nothing is decided from the earlier tenant context, the frontend or a stale loaded row.
- **Last owner.** Demotion, removal and leave that would leave zero owners are refused with one stable `409 last_owner`. Database backstop (migration `d18b4c6e2f31`): deferred constraint triggers on `organization_users` (DELETE, and UPDATE of role/organization) that fire only for a row that WAS an owner, count that organization's owners under a per-organization advisory lock, and refuse zero; a legacy organization that already has no owner stays valid and its unrelated changes are unaffected.
- **Removal and leave** delete the membership only: the user, their sessions, their other memberships and historical actor references (`invoices.issued_by`) stay; the next request to that organization is the ordinary tenant 404.
- **Security events** `member_role_changed`, `member_removed`, `member_left`, `owner_repaired` (actor, organization, target user id and roles in a short `detail`; no emails, names or bodies).
- **Operator repair** `python -m app.scripts.repair owner --organization-id ... --email ...`: makes an EXISTING member the owner of an organization that has none; never creates a membership, refuses an organization that has an owner; no HTTP path.
- **Members screen** `/o/{orgId}/members` (navigation entry and screen for owners and admins only; presentation) with role controls and confirmed removal, and **Leave organization** in the shell header for every role. After any answer the list is re-read from the server; refusals are shown as refusals (never as success), so a stale screen settles into the truth.
- **Tests.** Backend: the full authority matrix (every actor/target/role combination) under both the dev identity and real sessions with CSRF, self rules, leave, last owner, freshness against stale loaded rows, tenancy with look-alike organizations, removal side effects, events, the trigger with raw SQL, the operator repair, the migration (up/down/up with events and a legacy organization), and committed-data concurrency with real connections (mutual demotion/removal/leave, remove versus demote, promotion versus leave, leave versus removal, role change versus removal, gated stale-authority races, per-organization lock isolation, a mixed burst, the raw-SQL write skew, a legacy organization at a real commit). Frontend: presentation matrix, stale-UI messages, leave. Playwright in the dev and session runs (roles, flows, stale screens, last owner, look-alike organizations, CSRF layers, sessions surviving removal). Fault injection on the authority, lock, last-owner, tenancy, removal, event and trigger boundaries (reported with the S4 review).
- **Production authentication, S3: organization onboarding** (membership administration and invitations are later slices). An authenticated user whose account has `users.can_create_organizations` creates an organization and becomes its owner: `POST /api/organizations` inserts the organization, the owner membership (role `owner`, user = the authenticated user), a retry record and a security event in ONE database transaction (savepoint, then a single commit; no compensating deletion, no background task). ### Security
- Invitation tokens are 256-bit bearer secrets stored only as SHA-256, returned once, carried in a URL fragment and POST bodies only, and never in logs, events, lists, storage or redirects; acceptance as an existing account needs the invited email, never changes an existing membership, and settles under locks taken in one order shared with membership administration.
- Membership mutations authorize from fresh locked database rows only, never from the tenant context captured earlier, the frontend or a stale ORM object; an organization that has an owner can no longer lose its last one through the application or through raw SQL (deferred trigger), and an admin can never touch an owner or another admin or grant admin/owner.
- Organization creation judges `can_create_organizations` from the database under a user-row lock (never from a stale loaded user), takes the owner only from the authenticated user, and commits the organization and its owner together or not at all; the BFF door for it applies Origin and CSRF checks, forwards no organization, owner or role, and validates the retry key.
- In session mode the browser cannot assert identity, organization authority or role: the BFF builds upstream authentication from the HttpOnly session cookie only, validates Origin against the canonical `PUBLIC_ORIGIN` and the double-submit CSRF pair before forwarding, and FastAPI independently binds the CSRF token to the session. Login failures are one generic message, the setup-link secret never appears in a URL, header, storage or the DOM, login destinations are validated against an allowlist, and nothing from an upstream error can reach the browser as a secret.
- Authentication secrets are never stored or recorded: sessions, CSRF and setup tokens exist in plain text only in the one response or CLI output that shows them, passwords only as Argon2id hashes, and security events carry no password, token or email. Login failures are indistinguishable and equally expensive, throttling cannot be turned into an account lockout, hashing capacity is bounded with immediate refusal beyond it, and the development identity cannot be reached outside `APP_ENV=development` or alongside session mode.
- A stored invoice PDF cannot be changed or removed, even by raw SQL (trigger; the ORM also refuses), and only an issued invoice can have one. The download is tenant-scoped like every invoice read (a foreign or random id, draft or issued, is the same 404), and nothing is generated on behalf of a caller who cannot read the invoice.
- Stored text is treated as hostile: it is cleaned (control, invisible and bidirectional-override characters removed) and escaped before ReportLab's markup parser sees it, the PDF contains no links, scripts, forms, attachments or external references, nothing is fetched because of what a text says, and no system font is ever read. The download filename is derived from the invoice number alone and sanitized independently of the content.
- The BFF's binary pass-through validates what it relays (status, exact content type, magic, length, ETag equal to the body's SHA-256), rebuilds its headers instead of copying the backend's, and refuses a PDF content type on every other path.
- Issued invoices are immutable at the database level (triggers) as well as in the application; their sources are protected from deletion by tenant-safe RESTRICT references; invoice creation takes row locks on the source transactions in a fixed order and verifies everything under them.
- Organization settings can be changed only by owners and admins, enforced by the server (the UI only hides the form); the organization is never taken from the request, so a change can only reach the caller's own organization, and a foreign organization id is the same 404 as a random one.
- A transaction's currency cannot be changed once set, even by raw SQL (trigger), and the migration does not assign a currency to existing financial data, so no stored price is silently reinterpreted.
- The default currency cannot be changed while items or transactions exist; the check and the creation of prices serialize on the organization row, so a concurrent creation cannot slip past it.
- The browser never calls FastAPI and never sends identity or organization headers: the BFF builds backend requests from scratch, validates only shapes (UUID, known API areas, safe path segments, same origin, JSON bodies) and leaves membership decisions to FastAPI, which answers 404 for organizations the user does not belong to.
- Tenant-owned records can no longer have their `organization_id` changed after creation (ORM guard); Customer, Item and Horse requests containing `organization_id` are rejected. Cross-tenant references from horses to customers are rejected by both the API and PostgreSQL composite foreign keys.
